## Contract choice

Preserving the strict monitor is defensible only with an explicit compatibility limitation: legitimate short-lived children can prevent Stage A, and repeated passes cannot establish portability across scheduler timing. Continuous executable identity and descendant exclusion are not provided by these sampled observations anyway. Pursuing them through successive display exceptions exceeds the stated scientific need and consumes effort without delivering that stronger guarantee.

Prefer one lifecycle attribution mechanism for the finite source-owned families: ordinary observer ps, numeric cleanup ps, and the two exact authority Git commands. Keep their distinct vectors, owners, phase constraints and operation semantics. Source review must establish that declarations and ends arise from the same actual retained `Popen` object; JSON identifiers alone are not ownership. Supervisor-owned and parent-reported evidence must remain explicitly distinguishable. Extend the same conceptual attribution rule to the single clean reader launch, while preserving its stronger baseline and isolation requirements. Keep the accepted owned-root receipt route unchanged.

The bounded rules should be:

1. Bind unique launch ID, PID, parent generation, exact argv/hash, prelaunch artifact, family, source journal/offset, launch interval and actual owning completion. Validate worker/reader parent provenance from retained qualified ownership, without demanding that every asynchronous sample catch the parent live again. Authority phase and reader candidate/deadline bounds remain mandatory.
2. Permit genuine ordered native ESRCH with arbitrary retained display and nonnegative integer RSS when an unambiguous sample interval overlaps that exact lifecycle. Require matching actual reap/completion before final attribution; pending evidence gets no certification. Fast helpers need no previously sampled live image. A successful contradictory image, malformed lookup, ambiguous row or invalid chronology still fails. Record `source_owned_lifecycle` and `image_unavailable`, never `kernel_image` without its actual observation.
3. Treat intervals honestly: sample20 overlaps completion; neither its positive RSS nor sample21's earlier interval contradicts that completion. Arrival order must not substitute for observation time. Preserve both rows and peaks. Do not admit an arbitrarily later row through a stale declaration, manufacture an exit instant from completion_end, or introduce a grace window. Interval overlap supports source attribution under this trust model; it is not adversarial PID-reuse exclusion.
4. Globally reject duplicate PID/launch IDs, declared PID reuse, cross-family claims, mismatched parents/artifacts/vectors, contradictory ends and source gaps. An unknown child remains unresolved only through the existing bounded evidence drain, then stops; resemblance to a helper grants nothing. Never clear established contradictions when later evidence arrives.
5. Charge all observed helper RSS fully, including stale positive rows and numeric cleanup observations, with zero baseline credit and unchanged peak aggregation/caps. A never-observed fast helper has no measured RSS peak; successful attribution does not imply zero cost or continuous peak capture. For readers, lifecycle attribution alone must not grant baseline subtraction: retain genuine SELF/live-image/baseline qualification; missing qualification means full charge or failure under the existing resource policy. Preserve clean process launch, closed numeric request, result validation and reader-count gates.
6. End evidence proves owning completion, not scientific success. Future helper ends should retain actual returncode; nonzero/timeout still fails the operation even when its resources are attributed. Existing missing statuses stay missing. Truncated evidence, missing mandatory end, deadline/storage/cap violations and cleanup survivors retain failure. No extra wait, retry, budget or raw-evidence reduction is justified.
